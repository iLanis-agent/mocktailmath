Mocktail math

The good jug runs out three guests before the toast, and the top-up batch never tastes the same. Mocktail math scales a recipe to the whole party in liters, predicts how much the ice will water the glass down, and splits a pour into base, syrup and citrus by ratio. Exact arithmetic, every borrowed number labeled. Zero alcohol, all flavor.

The three tools

  • Batch scale - a recipe written for two glasses becomes the jug for the whole party, in ml and liters.
  • Ice melt - glass size, ice weight and melt guess become the final pour and the water percentage.
  • Ratio pour - base:syrup:citrus parts become exact ml for any glass size.

Labeled norms

1 g of ice melts to about 1 ml of water. Melt percent is your estimate - a cool room with big cubes might see 20-40%, a hot garden with crushed ice 60-100%. Bands under 10% water read as barely softened, 10-20% as a classic chill, above 20% as watery. These are taste heuristics, not bar law - your ice, your glass and your tongue win.
